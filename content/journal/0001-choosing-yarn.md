If you've ever wondered why your amigurumi looks a little different from the photo on the pattern, the answer is usually sitting right there in your hands: the yarn.

## Cotton

Cotton is a bit firmer and rougher on your hands, so it's a good idea to keep them moisturized while you work with this type of yarn. It's perfect for small details like embroidered noses, eyes, whiskers, and eyelashes. The stitches stay tight with very little stretch, which gives the piece a more structured shape.

## Acrylic

Acrylic sits somewhere in the middle. It's usually a bit cheaper, which makes it great if you're still finding your way into this craft. Pay attention though, because the fabric is more flexible with this yarn, so you need a bit more control with your hook. It's easier for the piece to come out a little "wonky" with acrylic.

## Polyester

Polyester is the gentlest on your hands. It's a very soft yarn that gives the finished amigurumi a fuller, plusher look, and it's my go-to these days. Pieces come out wonderfully soft, though the yarn also sheds quite a bit. There are also a few tricks to working with it, so even though it's comfortable to use, I'd recommend getting comfortable with the basics on other yarns first before jumping into something like chenille.

## Don't skip the TEX

Another important thing to check when picking your yarn is the TEX value. It's a number that tells you how thick the yarn is, and you'll usually find it on the label near the color code and hook recommendation. This small detail can make a big difference in your finished piece, since it determines the size and shape of your amigurumi.

Be careful not to make a common mistake, one I've made myself: using two yarns with different TEX values in the same piece. It's not a crime, and depending on the effect you're going for, it can even work well. But when it happens by accident, the piece can end up misshapen.

## Swatch before you commit

My biggest tip when choosing your yarn is to always make a small swatch first, a few rows with different stitches, to see how that yarn behaves. It saves you both materials and time.

Good luck! Whatever you choose, I'm sure it'll turn out lovely. Don't forget to have fun and relax through the process. See you next time!
