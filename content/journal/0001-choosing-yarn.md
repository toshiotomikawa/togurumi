If you've ever wondered why your amigurumi looks a little different from the photo on the pattern, the answer is usually sitting right there in your hands: the yarn.

## Cotton

Cotton is my go-to for most amigurumi. It has almost no stretch, which means your stitches stay put and the fabric holds its shape — perfect for tiny ears, noses, and anything that needs crisp definition. The trade-off is that cotton is a little tougher on the hands over a long session, and it doesn't have much "give," so gauge matters more.

## Acrylic

Acrylic is soft, cheap, and comes in every color imaginable, which makes it tempting for a first project. But it's springier than cotton, so stitches can look slightly looser and the fabric drapes more. For a snuggly, huggable toy that's exactly what you want. For a character with sharp features, it can blur the details a bit.

## Same pattern, two yarns

I made the same little fox pattern twice — once in worsted cotton, once in acrylic — and they came out like two different animals. The cotton fox looked alert and a bit formal, every stitch visible. The acrylic fox looked soft and round, like it wanted a hug immediately.

Neither is "better." It just depends on what you're making and who it's for. For my first amigurumi, I'd actually recommend a cotton-acrylic blend — you get some of the stitch definition of cotton with the squish of acrylic, and it's gentler on beginner hands.

Whatever you pick, swatch a few rounds first. It's the cheapest way to avoid being three rows into an ear before realizing the yarn had other plans.
