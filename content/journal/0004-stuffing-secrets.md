Stuffing is the part of amigurumi nobody warns you about, and it can quietly make or break a finished piece. Two dolls made from the exact same pattern can feel completely different depending on how — and what — you stuff them with.

## Polyfill is the default for a reason

Polyester fiberfill is light, washable, and holds its shape well. For most bodies and heads, it's exactly what you want. The trick is adding it in small pinches rather than big handfuls — small pieces fill gaps evenly and avoid the lumpy, asymmetrical look that comes from one giant clump shifting around inside.

## Scrap yarn for "weight" and structure

For pieces that need to sit upright or feel a bit more substantial — like a seated bear or a doll that should "stand" — I tuck a few wound-up balls of scrap yarn into the base before adding polyfill around them. It adds just enough weight and a firmer core without making the whole piece heavy or stiff.

## The chopstick is non-negotiable

A blunt chopstick (or the back of a crochet hook) is the single most useful stuffing tool I own. Fingers can't reach into narrow limbs, ears, or tails — but a chopstick can pack stuffing right into the tip, which is the difference between a floppy, deflated-looking ear and one that holds its shape.

## Firm vs. soft, on purpose

Here's the part that's actually about *feel*, not technique: bodies and limbs usually want to be on the firmer side, so the toy holds its pose and doesn't sag. But cheeks, bellies, and anything meant to be squished or hugged should be left noticeably softer — under-stuff slightly, then check by giving it an actual hug. If it feels nice to squeeze, it's done.

That last step is unscientific, but it's never steered me wrong.
