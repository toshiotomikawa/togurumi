Every custom amigurumi I make starts the same way: a messy doodle in the corner of a notebook, usually drawn while I'm supposed to be doing something else.

## The doodle isn't the pattern

The hardest lesson I've learned is that a cute drawing doesn't automatically become a cute crochet shape. Drawings can have impossibly thin limbs, huge heads on tiny bodies, or expressions made entirely of line weight — none of which translate directly into rounds of single crochet.

So the first step is always a "translation" pass: which features are essential to the character's personality, and which ones can flex? Usually it's two or three things — for one recent commission, it was the character's oversized ears and the slightly lopsided smile. Everything else (body shape, proportions, even the tail) I had room to adjust for what crochet does well.

## Building in rounds, not lines

Crochet amigurumi is built from spheres, cylinders, and cones — increasing and decreasing stitch counts to shape fabric in 3D. So instead of thinking "draw an ear," I think "what increase pattern gives me a curved triangle about this size?" It's a bit like sculpting with math, except the math is mostly counting to six.

## Prototyping is non-negotiable

I always make at least one rough prototype before using "good" yarn. The prototype almost never looks right on the first try — heads are too big, arms are too long, ears flop the wrong way. But each prototype gets the silhouette closer, and by the second or third try, the little doodle from my notebook starts looking back at me.

That moment — when a pile of stitches suddenly looks like *someone* — is still my favorite part of this whole hobby.
