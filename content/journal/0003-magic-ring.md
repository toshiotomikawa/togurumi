The magic ring (or "magic circle") is the very first thing most amigurumi patterns ask you to do, and for a long time it was the thing that almost made me quit before I started. Mine kept coming undone — sometimes immediately, sometimes three rows later, which is somehow worse.

If your magic rings keep unraveling, here's everything that eventually fixed mine.

## 1. Leave a longer tail than feels necessary

I used to leave a tiny 2-inch tail because it seemed tidy. Don't. A 6-inch tail gives you something real to grip when you pull the ring closed, and plenty of length to weave in securely afterward.

## 2. Pull the *short end*, not the working yarn

This one took embarrassingly long to click. To close the ring, you pull the **tail end** — not the strand attached to your ball of yarn. Pulling the wrong one just tightens your stitches around a still-open loop.

## 3. Pull it closed *before* you panic-weave the tail in

Finish all your stitches in round one, then pull the tail to cinch the ring shut, then move on. If you weave the tail in too early, there's nothing left to pull.

## 4. When in doubt, use a chain-2 start instead

If a magic ring genuinely won't behave with your yarn (slippery acrylics are the worst offenders), there's no shame in starting with a chain of 2 and working your stitches into the second chain from the hook. The hole in the middle is slightly bigger, but it's reliable — and a few sts later, nobody can tell the difference.

## 5. Practice on scrap yarn, not your good stuff

I made probably fifteen magic rings on cheap scrap yarn before I trusted myself to do it on a "real" project. Worth it.

These days my magic rings hold just fine — though I'll admit I still give the tail one extra tug for good luck.
